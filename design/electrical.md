# Electrical supply from the turbines to the racks

**Design basis for owner review · 25 November 2026.** Normal operation is an island with up to 50 MW of site-rated turbine generation, a 4 MW allowance for plant and losses, and a 46 MW IT ceiling. Either cooling conditions or available generation can lower that ceiling. A 6 MW intertie is available as an option subject to a utility agreement. The four diesels serve emergencies. This note records equipment duties and the owner's preferred packaging for supplier coordination. ## Grey service section of each 4SU

Reserve the grey section at the wall end for the pod's rack-power interface alongside its cooling distribution unit (CDU) and pump. Its **gross external envelope is 2.16 m long × 0.60 m wide × 2.30 m high**, approximately 2.98 m³ before skins, structure, pipework and maintenance access. The complete operating pod is currently calculated at **8.93 t**. That includes 0.60 t for the CDU and pump and 0.40 t for local pipework and liquid. The structure has no separately demonstrated mass allowance for the proposed electrical package. Additional mass at the wall end has the greatest leverage on the cantilever. | --- | --- |
| Rack load and primary contact | 4 × 195.3125 kW = 781.25 kW. At assumed three-phase power factor 0.95 this is 0.822 MVA, about 475 A at 1 kV or 989 A at 480 V. Adding the 8.2 kW CDU pump on the same feeder gives about 480 A or 1,000 A respectively, before converter losses. Carry the pump on essential power where ride-through requires it. |
| Local conversion and board | Seek a continuous 1.0 MVA, 1 kV to 480 V three-phase package with a dry compartment, input isolation/protection, output protection, bonded enclosure and per-rack metering. At full rating it draws 577 A at 1 kV and delivers 1,203 A at 480 V. Study a 1,250–1,600 A secondary board and confirm continuous duty at the installed temperature. At 415 V the four racks alone draw approximately 1,144 A. |
| Connections inside the pod | NVIDIA's DGX reference has four 110 kW shelves per rack, each with two 100 A AC whips. Allow routes and terminations for eight whips per rack or 32 per pod if that rack is purchased. Select branch protection, cable count, neutral, earthing arrangement and availability against the actual rack schedule. Shelf capacity and cord ratings describe the interface. Use metered whole-rack input for dispatch. |
| Added heat | At an illustrative 98.5–99% efficiency, converting 781.25 kW produces about 8–12 kW of heat per loaded pod. At 46 MW of rack load the corresponding loss is 0.46–0.70 MW. The existing 3.78 MW auxiliary subtotal already includes a 1.6% electrical loss allowance and leaves only about 0.22 MW to the 4 MW budget. Reconcile the losses of every stage at a common meter boundary and lower the operating cap if necessary. |

The rack power basis comes from a thermal design allowance and needs confirmation as metered AC input, including power shelves and standby demand. **Packaging decision.** Seek a supplier arrangement for all pod-level functions inside the existing grey envelope. Require dimensioned sections showing the occupied wet equipment, a dry barrier and drainage, cable bends, cooling, removal paths, access to terminals, operating mass, centre of gravity, transport and lift reactions. As a packaging comparison, Eaton’s 1 MVA SCB10 in its 10 kV range measures 1.70 × 0.85 × 1.55 m and weighs 3.32 t even without an enclosure. Its voltage ratio differs from the proposed 1 kV unit. Its bare width already exceeds the grey pod body's 0.60 m. A compact or modular proposal needs its own evidence. The original 7.5 MVA transformers and the essential UPS stay on independent ground supports. [Eaton dry-type dimensions, 10 kV table](https://www.eaton.com/content/dam/eaton/products/utility-and-grid-solutions/transformer/dry-type-transformer/Eaton-Dry-Type-Transformer-Brochure-EN-US.pdf)

If local conversion exceeds the verified space, wet mass or heat budget, place conversion on fixed supports and retain local isolation, protection and rack distribution in the grey section. Moving the voltage step ahead of the dock changes its current. At 480 V four racks need about 1 kA, above a single 630 A contact. Study a suitably rated 1,250–1,600 A interface or two separately protected, interlocked 630 A circuits, each feeding two racks at about 495 A. Two load-sharing circuits provide no full-load redundancy by themselves. Both arrangements require opening the supplying breaker(s), proving dead and retracting the contact(s) before indexing, with earth first to make and last to break.

## Fixed plant, controls and emergencies

| Equipment | Quantity and preliminary duty | Design evidence still needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generator and MV switchgear | Two generator-duty breakers, two 13.8 kV bus sections, a controlled tie, eight transformer feeders, and separate connections for station services, emergency sources and any utility intertie. Each 25 MW machine supplies about 1,101 A at 13.8 kV and 0.95 power factor. Two 1,600 A shaft buses are modelled. One such bus cannot carry the full two-machine output. | Guarantee continuous propane output and reactive capability at site altitude and ambient temperature. Select generator voltage, grounding, bus tie operating states and breaker interrupting duty. A generator step transformer is needed only if the alternator voltage differs from the bus voltage. |
| Bay transformation | Eight × 7.5 MVA, 13.8/1.0 kV. At rating each unit draws 314 A at 13.8 kV and delivers 4,330 A at 1 kV. Study 400 A primary feeders and 5,000 A class secondary boards with eight 630 A branches each. | Cable grouping, voltage drop, transformer impedance, cooling, protection selectivity, inrush and access. The total installed transformer capacity is 60 MVA. A failed bay still requires an alternate route or shedding. |
| Station services | Study two approximately 4 MVA station-service trains. A 3 MW essential load at 0.90 power factor requires 3.33 MVA. Schedule each essential feeder and determine how it transfers. Provide motor control centres and drives for 64 pod pumps, 30 cooler pumps and 30 dry-cooler fan groups. | Electrical motor duties, simultaneous starts, harmonic currents, motor ride-through, emergency cooling sequence, breaker selectivity and the actual essential load list. The 4 MW normal allowance includes distribution losses as well as connected auxiliaries. |
| Emergency and UPS | Four 2.5 MW diesel sets. At 0.80 generator power factor each requires 3.125 MVA alternator capacity. Provide a breaker per set and synchronising/load-sharing controls. If low-voltage alternators supply the MV bus, allow a matching step-up for each, approximately 3.15–3.75 MVA subject to selection. The 10 MW / 1.25 MWh essential UPS needs rectifier/inverter modules, batteries, bypass and segregated feeders. | Document how essential IT and circulation remain energised through source changeover. At 10 MW, five minutes is 0.833 MWh AC. At 80% availability the battery allowance is 1.0 MWh before conversion. Verify end-of-life power, losses, temperature and the diesel start/pickup sequence. |
| Plant controls and protection | Governors share real power and regulate island frequency. Automatic voltage regulators share reactive power and control voltage. The plant power-management system checks synchronism, authorises breaker closing, caps rack demand, stages motors and sheds loads on a generator trip. Provide CTs/VTs, protection relays, trip/control DC, utility protection where applicable, and independent hardwired trips. | Study islanded and grid-connected short-circuit current, earth faults, generator and transformer protection, arc flash, selectivity, load steps and black start. Obtain utility fault duty and generator subtransient reactance before specifying a breaker kA rating. Verify generator fuel control and shutdown independently with the propane package supplier. |

At two site-rated turbines the normal IT ceiling is 46 MW. One turbine leaves at most 21 MW IT with the same 4 MW plant allowance. On losing both turbines, shed to a maximum of 7 MW priority IT plus 3 MW essential plant once all four emergency sets are available. With only three sets, 7.5 MW gross leaves at most 4.5 MW for priority IT at that plant load, before any further derating or load-step margin. The 10 MW UPS bridges the selected essential load only. The 76-minute thermal storage result also requires pumps and controls to remain powered.
